# Taller Milenio Analytics — consulting delivery kit

This repository is an MIT-licensed, local-first analytics toolkit. The license
grants permission to use, copy, modify, and distribute the code under the MIT
notice. Synthetic examples are deliberately labelled and do not describe
Taller Milenio's real performance. A client engagement needs a separate written
scope for access, confidentiality, ownership of supplied data, and deliverable
use.

The service menu is intentionally scoped:

| Offering | Deliverable | Acceptance evidence | Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data readiness diagnostic | source inventory, field map, quality gaps, consent/anonymization decisions | client confirms inventory and open questions | sample exports and process owner interviews |
| Operational analytics pilot | reconciled snapshot, exception queue, KPI definitions, decision/action tracker | agreed fields reconcile; report reruns from supplied snapshot | stable export cadence and named reviewers |
| Recurring operating review | dated weekly packet, trend comparison, unresolved evidence log, action follow-up | owner reviews packet and records disposition | fresh export, cutoff, owners, and approval log |

These deliverables support measurable validation such as row coverage, reference
integrity, reconciliation checks, freshness, exception closure, and review
completion. They do not guarantee revenue, ROI, utilization, conversion, SLA
improvement, or collections. No market price is invented here; price and scope
are agreed in the engagement, and no price is changed during a workshop.

The toolkit does not dispatch tow units, diagnose vehicles, authorize repairs,
send prospect messages, collect payment, issue fiscal documents, or make a
safety decision. Public B2B research remains a human-reviewed research queue.
Real data requires consent, minimization, anonymization where appropriate,
source ownership, retention rules, and a written acceptance boundary.

## Delivery sequence

1. Start with the data-request checklist and record missing fields, owners,
   freshness, and consent decisions.
2. Run the local import preview against synthetic or authorized exports. Keep
   the source snapshot immutable and preserve the preview errors.
3. Rerun the reports from a fixed cutoff. Record evidence IDs in the action
   tracker and approval log; leave outcomes unknown until observed.
4. Hold the weekly review and update owner, due date, review status, and realized
   outcome with dated evidence.
5. Use the handover checklist to record acceptance, limitations, open decisions,
   and the next export owner.

The current repository demonstrates the mechanics with synthetic fixtures. The
real process, source system grain, freshness, consent, and business outcomes
remain to be observed and accepted by the client.
